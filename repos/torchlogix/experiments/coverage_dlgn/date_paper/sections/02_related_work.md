# Related Work

Research on DLGNs has addressed the design of the network architecture, the selection of gate inputs, and the optimization of Boolean functions. Deep DLGNs introduced gradient-based gate learning with fixed random connections, followed by discretization for Boolean inference [@petersen2022]. Convolutional DLGNs extended this approach with shared logic trees, pooling, and residual initialization to exploit image structure [@petersen2024]. Studies of output aggregation further show that temperature and classifier design affect scaling to larger class counts [@brandle2025]. These architectural choices determine how signals are represented and combined; LogicConnect designs the connections within a given dense or convolutional architecture.

Learning the connections provides another means of improving the combinations available to each gate. Mommen et al. optimize distributions over candidate predecessors, allowing input selection to change during training [@mommen2025]. LILogic Net combines sparse Top-K routing with basis projection to improve compact logic networks [@fojcik2026]. The resulting accuracy gains are accompanied by additional training state for candidate selection. LogicConnect addresses this trade-off through an offline construction whose connections remain fixed during gate learning. We therefore compare the methods in terms of both classification accuracy and the memory, parameter count, and time required for training.

A complementary line of work improves the optimization of the gate functions themselves. Mind the Gap uses Gumbel noise and a straight-through estimator to reduce the loss caused by discretization [@yousefi2025]. Light DLGNs modify the gate parameterization, WARP represents Boolean functions in a Walsh–Hadamard basis, and multilinear polynomial fitting examines redundant optimization directions in soft gate mixtures [@ruttgers2025; gerlach2026; kim2026]. These methods act on the functions computed from the assigned inputs. Our main experiments hold the gate-training rule fixed to isolate connectivity, while additional WARP experiments examine its compatibility with an alternative parameterization.

Connectivity also interacts with broader choices in logic-network design. BitLogic studies encoding, connectivity, fan-in, and training within a framework for lookup-table-native networks [@buehrer2026]. Unit tying reduces trained circuit size through functional approximation and refinement [@lee2026]. These approaches concern a broader design space or a later optimization stage than the fixed-budget construction studied here.
