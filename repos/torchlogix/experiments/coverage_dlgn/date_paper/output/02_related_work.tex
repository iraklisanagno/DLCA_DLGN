\section{Related Work}

\textbf{Differentiable logic architectures.} Petersen et al. train LGNs through continuous relaxations of Boolean functions, then discretize each gate for inference \cite{petersen2022}. The connection graph remains independent of the learned gate functions, separating graph construction from the subsequent optimization of Boolean operations. Convolutional LGNs extend this architecture with shared logic trees, OR pooling, and residual initialization \cite{petersen2024}. These architectural changes improve spatial processing while retaining a separate choice of channel connectivity that determines access within receptive fields. We study this choice under matched architectural budgets, preserving the corresponding dense and convolutional operators throughout each paired comparison.

\textbf{Learning the connections.} Mommen et al. optimize distributions over subsets of candidate inputs to improve the connections available to each gate \cite{mommen2025}. LILogic Net extends learnable connectivity with a sparse Top-K mechanism and efficient evaluation through basis projection \cite{fojcik2026}. These approaches address the limitations of fixed random graphs by training routing decisions, whereas our construction determines the graph before optimization. The relevant comparison includes both accuracy and training resources, since additional candidate signals can consume memory even when the deployed graph remains compact.

\textbf{Separating the design axes.} BitLogic makes this comparison broader by organizing LUT-native networks along axes that include encoding, connectivity, fan-in, and training choices \cite{buehrer2026}. Since a gain obtained by changing several axes cannot identify the effect of connectivity alone, we use matched random/U2 pairs for attribution. Comparisons involving different architectures or routing mechanisms retain explicit labels so that their accuracy gains do not imply a topology-only effect. Published accuracies provide context, while locally measured accuracy, memory, and wall time establish the trade-off under our execution conditions.

\textbf{Gate parameterization.} Light DLGNs and WARP change the representation used to train gate functions \cite{ruttgers2025,gerlach2026}. Such representations affect parameter count, optimization, and hardening, addressing a different design variable from the predecessor indices studied here. Nevertheless, the resulting optimization behavior can interact with connectivity, making compatibility a question that requires evaluation under each gate representation. Our adapted WARP controls therefore test compatibility explicitly instead of assuming that a positive result with the original gate parameterization transfers to every alternative.

\textbf{Simplifying trained circuits.} Two-stage unit tying reduces the logical footprint of a trained LGN through functional approximation and subsequent refinement \cite{lee2026}. Unit tying changes the retained structure after training, whereas U2 constructs a graph at a declared budget before training. Both choices can matter to a deployment flow, but evaluating their joint benefit requires measurements beyond nominal gate count, including physical area and energy.

Together, these studies motivate a comparison that keeps the graph, the gate-training rule, and the deployment representation distinct. Our contribution is a shared fixed-connectivity construction with measured accuracy/resource trade-offs and controlled structural ablations that preserve local predecessor degrees. The following methodology defines the information used during construction, the pair-selection rule, and the indices retained by the trained model.
