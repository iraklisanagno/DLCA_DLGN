Logic gate networks learn Boolean functions on a fixed graph, making connectivity a design choice that directly constrains the signals available to each gate. We present a unified methodology for constructing this graph in dense and convolutional logic gate networks. The method combines semantic input pairing with deterministic multiscale matching stages, prioritizing balanced predecessor use and using ancestry overlap to select among equally balanced stages. Construction occurs before training and adds neither trainable routing parameters nor inference operators. Experiments on MNIST, Fashion-MNIST, and CIFAR-10 measure hardened accuracy and training cost under matched architectural budgets. On dense CIFAR-10 models, the unified construction improves mean test accuracy by 3.18-4.59 percentage points across three paired seeds. Convolutional S and M models gain 3.26 and 2.08 points in single-seed tests, although additional S replications yield an inconclusive three-seed validation improvement. Degree-preserving ablations identify first-layer structure as the dominant contributor in the evaluated dense model. The results support offline connectivity design as a practical accuracy and training-resource trade-off.
