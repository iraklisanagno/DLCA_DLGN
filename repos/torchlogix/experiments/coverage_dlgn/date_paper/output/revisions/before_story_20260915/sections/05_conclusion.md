# Conclusion and Discussion

We presented LogicConnect, a unified construction that organizes connectivity before gate-function training in dense and convolutional LGNs. Semantic pairing preserves the meaning of image inputs, while deeper matching stages provide deterministic multiscale connections with controlled predecessor reuse. The convolutional adapter applies the same construction to channel groups and the classifier while preserving spatial sampling. The trained model retains ordinary fixed indices and gate functions, with no additional routing parameters or inference operators.

The dense results establish the value of this design choice under the evaluated budgets. U2 improves all three CIFAR-10 sizes over matched random graphs, and the factorial study identifies first-layer structure as the dominant contributor at dense M. However, the available controls do not isolate ancestry novelty as the source of improvement, and native random connectivity already balances fan-out. The additional contribution of deeper structure remains uncertain, even though the full construction attains the highest mean in the dense factorial study. The method should therefore be understood as a tested construction, rather than a proven coverage-maximization principle.

The convolutional evidence requires a narrower interpretation because architectural compatibility and replicated accuracy gains answer different experimental questions. Single-seed held-out improvements demonstrate feasible application to both S and M, but full-S validation replications do not establish a consistent gain. The transfer experiments reinforce the need to preserve this distinction when moving beyond the original training distribution or class count. A shared construction reduces the number of design rules, while its empirical value remains specific to the evaluated architecture and workload.

For deployment-oriented design, U2 offers a measured alternative when training memory matters and fixed architectural budgets are required. Learned routing can remain more accurate, while unchanged logical cost leaves physical area, delay, and energy dependent on the eventual implementation. Additional held-out convolutional replications and matched synthesis are therefore needed to establish the corresponding accuracy and physical implementation trade-offs. The supported design insight is that organizing accessible signals before optimization can improve the accuracy obtained from a fixed logic graph.
