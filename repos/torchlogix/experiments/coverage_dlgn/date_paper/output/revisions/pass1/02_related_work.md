# Related Work

**Differentiable logic architectures.** Petersen et al. train LGNs through continuous relaxations of Boolean functions, then discretize each gate for inference [@petersen2022]. Their construction makes the connection graph independent of the learned function at each gate. Convolutional LGNs extend this approach with shared logic trees, OR pooling, and residual initialization [@petersen2024]. These architectural changes improve spatial processing, but they retain a separate choice of channel connectivity. We preserve the dense and convolutional operators and study that connectivity choice under matched architectural budgets.

**Learning the connections.** Mommen et al. optimize distributions over subsets of candidate inputs to improve the connections available to each gate [@mommen2025]. LILogic Net extends learnable connectivity with a sparse Top-K mechanism and efficient evaluation through basis projection [@fojcik2026]. These approaches address the limitations of fixed random graphs by training the routing decisions. Our construction instead determines the graph before optimization. The relevant comparison includes both accuracy and training resources, since additional candidate signals can consume memory even when the deployed graph remains compact.

**Separating the design axes.** BitLogic makes this comparison broader by organizing LUT-native networks along axes that include encoding, connectivity, fan-in, and training choices [@buehrer2026]. A gain obtained by changing several axes cannot identify the effect of connectivity alone. We therefore use matched random/U2 pairs for attribution and label comparisons with different architectures or routing mechanisms separately. Published accuracies provide context, while locally measured accuracy, memory, and wall time establish the trade-off under our execution conditions.

**Gate parameterization.** Light DLGNs and WARP change the representation used to train gate functions [@ruttgers2025; gerlach2026]. Such representations affect parameter count, optimization, and the conversion from relaxed outputs to Boolean gates. They address a different design variable from the predecessor indices studied here. Nevertheless, the resulting optimization behavior can interact with connectivity. Our adapted WARP controls therefore test compatibility explicitly instead of assuming that a positive result with the original gate parameterization transfers to every alternative.

**Simplifying trained circuits.** Two-stage unit tying reduces the logical footprint of a trained LGN through functional approximation and subsequent refinement [@lee2026]. This changes the retained structure after training, whereas U2 constructs a graph at a declared budget before training. Both choices can matter to a deployment flow, but their joint benefit requires a separate evaluation. We do not infer physical area or energy from a nominal gate count.

Together, these studies motivate a comparison that keeps the graph, the gate-training rule, and the deployment representation distinct. Our contribution is a shared fixed-connectivity construction with measured accuracy/resource trade-offs and controlled structural ablations.
